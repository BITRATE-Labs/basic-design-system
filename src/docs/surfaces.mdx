# Surfaces

Each brand picks a surface style in its brand file. **Flat** (the default) keeps stock shadcn surfaces: a 1px ring at 10% foreground and, on popups, `shadow-md`. **Glass** is Perkcord's lit-edge glass, and the perkcord brand uses it. The stories beside this page show both; switch light and dark from the toolbar.

## What glass changes

Glass replaces the stock ring with a real 1px border at 13% foreground and a brighter top edge, adds a faint 145° sheen in the top-left corner, a 1px inner highlight and a long shadow tinted green-grey instead of black. Where the browser can blur, it also makes the fill translucent and blurs what is behind it:

| Surface                                           | Fill (light, dark)         | Blur                        | Shadow              |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Card                                              | 36%, 44% of `--background` | `blur(7px) saturate(1.15)`  | `0 18px 52px -24px` |
| Dialog, AlertDialog, Sheet                        | 72%, 62% of `--background` | `blur(7px) saturate(1.15)`  | `0 18px 52px -24px` |
| DropdownMenu, Popover, Select and Combobox popups | 84% of `--popover`         | `blur(12px) saturate(1.15)` | `0 18px 44px -20px` |
| Tooltip                                           | opaque `--popover`         | none                        | `0 18px 44px -20px` |
| Card with `data-fill="uniform"`                   | 82% of `--background`      | as Card, no sheen           | as Card             |

The Tooltip takes the popover colours in a glass brand, as Perkcord's tooltip does, instead of stock's inverted fill; a `Kbd` inside it keeps its normal muted look.

Glass menus also take Perkcord's menu geometry and highlight: items 36px tall with 6px by 10px padding, a 10px gap and 6px corners, menus at least 176px wide, and a highlight of 10% primary (the system's wash elsewhere is 5%) with normal text. The destructive item keeps the system's soft wash, which is contrast-checked; coarse pointers still get 44px rows.

## The page backdrop

Glass only reads as glass when something is behind it. Put `data-surface-backdrop` on `<body>` or the app root to paint the brand's backdrop there (fixed to the viewport). For perkcord it is the product's community scene with synthetic art in place of its images: in light mode a pale blue-to-mint sky with drawn clouds, in dark mode a green night glow with a star field at the product's 0.57, and in both a ridge and two rows of spruces along the bottom 45vh at the product's forest opacity (0.16 light, 0.36 dark), all under a scrim of the page colour. The art is inline SVG in the token, so there is nothing to host. The blur needs that detail behind it: over a smooth gradient a translucent panel looks like a flat card. Flat brands ignore the attribute, so it is safe to set everywhere; Storybook sets it on every story.

```html
<html data-brand="perkcord">
  <body data-surface-backdrop>
    <div class="isolate">…</div>
  </body>
</html>
```

## Known contrast over the backdrop

axe cannot measure text on a gradient, so `tests/tokens.test.ts` composites the backdrop's extreme points from its stops and checks the roles used as page text. Perkcord's destructive red, already below 4.5:1 on its plain page (3.57:1), falls further over the scene. It stays as the product has it and is listed in the test's backdrop allowlist:

| Pair (perkcord light)                                   | Ratio  |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| `--destructive` text over the sky's top under the scrim | 2.88:1 |
| `--destructive` text over the tree line under the scrim | 2.72:1 |

Foreground, muted and primary text reach 4.5:1 at every point in both modes (primary lowest, 4.53:1 over the light tree line). Field errors and invalid labels are the affected text; on a card they sit on the card's fill instead.

## Long lists

Give a Card `data-fill="uniform"` when it holds a long list or a table: an even, mostly opaque fill with no sheen, so rows read alike from top to bottom.

## Fallbacks

The translucent fill and blur turn on only where `backdrop-filter` is supported and the person has not asked for reduced transparency (`prefers-reduced-transparency`), more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`) or forced colours. Otherwise every surface keeps the edge, sheen and shadow on an opaque fill. `data-transparency="reduce"` on `<html>` (or any ancestor of a surface) forces the same fallback, for an in-app setting; the **Glass Fallback** story shows it. With more contrast the backdrop becomes the plain page colour, and in forced colours the sheen and backdrop go and the real border keeps every edge visible (stock's ring is a box-shadow, which forced colours removes).

## Costs

Blur is expensive on long scrolling pages and where surfaces stack, and a blurred element becomes the containing block for its `position: fixed` descendants. Use the uniform fill for long content.

## Adding glass to a brand

Add a `surface` block to the brand file with `"style": { "$value": "glass" }` and a `light` and `dark` value for every key that `scripts/build-tokens.mjs` lists in `SURFACE_KEYS` (copy `brands/perkcord.tokens.json`). Each becomes a `--surface-<key>` property in the brand CSS, and the build scopes the glass rules in `dist/theme.css` to that brand. A missing key fails the build. Measure text contrast over the backdrop afterwards: translucent fills change it.

The rules sit in a `surfaces` cascade layer after Tailwind's, so they win over the stock utilities without `!important`; unlayered product CSS still wins over them.
